⚠ Hazard 5

Gas Cylinder Storage

Read through all sections to complete this module. Then test your knowledge in the Quizzes.

01

Why Proper Gas Cylinder Storage Matters

Hydrogen gas cylinders store gas under very high pressure, which means improper storage can create serious safety risks. A damaged or unsecured cylinder can lead to gas leaks, fire, explosion, or physical injury.

Safe storage helps reduce the risk of accidents and ensures cylinders remain stable and protected while not in use.

02

Common Storage Hazards

Unsafe cylinder storage can include:

  • Unsecured cylinders
  • Cylinders stored near heat or ignition sources
  • Damaged valves or regulators
  • Poor ventilation in storage areas
  • Cylinders placed in high-traffic areas
  • Storing incompatible gases together
💡 Even a small impact to a cylinder valve can cause dangerous gas release.
03

Safe Storage Practices

To safely store hydrogen gas cylinders:

  • Secure cylinders using chains or straps
  • Keep cylinders upright at all times
  • Store cylinders in well-ventilated areas
  • Keep protective valve caps in place when not in use
  • Store cylinders away from heat, sparks, and flames
  • Regularly inspect cylinders and fittings for damage
💡 Storage areas should also remain clean and free from obstacles.
04

Warning Signs of Unsafe Storage

Signs of unsafe cylinder storage may include:

  • Cylinders standing freely without restraints
  • Damaged valves or missing caps
  • Leaking fittings or hissing sounds
  • Cylinders stored near electrical equipment or heat sources
  • Visible corrosion or physical damage
💡 Any damaged or unstable cylinder should be reported immediately.
05

Emergency Response

If a cylinder leak or storage issue is identified:

  1. Keep away from ignition sources
  2. Alert nearby personnel
  3. Evacuate the area if necessary
  4. Inform trained laboratory staff or emergency responders
  5. Do not attempt to repair damaged cylinders unless properly trained

🔑 Key Takeaway

Hydrogen gas cylinders contain large amounts of stored energy and must be handled and stored carefully. Proper securing, ventilation, regular inspection, and safe storage practices are essential to prevent leaks, fires, and serious accidents in laboratory environments.
